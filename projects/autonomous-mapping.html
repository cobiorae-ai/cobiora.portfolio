Autonomous Mapping & Exploration

Building a spatial representation of unfamiliar environments and planning autonomous exploration with depth-camera measurements and sampling-based methods.

How can robots explore unknown space?
PROJECT DETAILS / 01

Overview

Robots operating in unfamiliar environments must discover obstacles and plan safe movements without a complete map. In a Mobile Robotics project at Carnegie Mellon University, I implemented a depth-camera simulation workflow to build three-dimensional occupancy maps and support autonomous exploration. I used log-odds updates to combine successive depth measurements into an occupancy representation, then worked with sampling-based planning approaches such as rapidly exploring random trees (RRT) to investigate candidate motions and exploration paths. The work included visualization and testing in simulated environments with different levels of complexity. These experiments illustrate the relationship between mapping quality, planning behavior, and the challenges of navigating unobserved space.

My Contributions

  • Implemented occupancy-grid updates from simulated depth-camera data.
  • Used log-odds representations to accumulate evidence about free and occupied space.
  • Developed or evaluated RRT-based exploration and path planning in simulated environments.
  • Visualized maps, sampled trees, and paths with Python and Open3D.

Design Process & Methods

The exploration workflow converts sensor measurements into a spatial map, uses that map to reason about reachable space, and checks planned routes in simulation.
